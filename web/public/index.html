How a circle works

Four moves. Zero gas.

  1. 01

    Organize

    Pick the amount, the members and the round length. Creating a circle is free; the relayer pays the gas.

  2. 02

    Join with a signature

    Your wallet signs a USDC authorization for the deposit plus a tiny fee. No gas token, no approval transaction.

  3. 03

    Pay each round

    Sign, done. When the last share arrives, the same transaction pays the whole pot to that round's member.

  4. 04

    Nobody stalls it

    Miss a deadline and anyone can settle: your share comes from your deposit and the pot still goes out. Deposits return at the end.

Why it has to be Arc

Dollars that are also the gas.

On Arc, USDC pays for the network. So a member's dollars are all they ever need, and the relayer can be repaid in the same dollars, out of the amount they signed.

Paying into a circle elsewhere
  1. 1Buy a separate gas token
  2. 2Approve USDC (a transaction)
  3. 3Send the contribution (another one)
  4. 4Wait for confirmations

Two volatile assets, two transactions, an anxious wait.

Paying into an Ajo circle on Arc
  1. 1Sign once. That's it.

The relayer submits it, keeps a 0.005 USDC fee from the signed amount (the contract caps it at 0.05), and the pot pays out in the same transaction.

Savings passport

Every share you pay is a stamp.

The contract keeps a record for every member: rounds paid, rounds missed, pots received, circles completed. It belongs to the saver, not a bank, and any lender or app can read it. A credit history for people banks ignore.
