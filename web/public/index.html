An ajo (esusu, susu, chama, tontine) is how millions of people save: everyone pays the same amount each round, and each round the whole pot goes to one member until everyone has had a turn. This one runs on Arc. Members hold only USDC, sign instead of paying gas, and the pot lands the moment the last share does.

How a circle works

  1. Organize. Pick the amount, the number of members and the round length. Creating a circle is free for you; the relayer pays the gas.
  2. Join with a signature. Your wallet signs a USDC authorization (EIP-3009) for the security deposit plus a tiny relay fee. No gas, no second token, no approval transaction.
  3. Contribute each round. Same thing: sign, done. When the last member's share arrives, the contract pays the whole pot to that round's member in the same transaction.
  4. Nobody stalls the circle. After the deadline anyone can settle the round: a missing share is covered from that member's deposit and the pot still goes out. When the last round pays out, every deposit goes back automatically.

Savings record

Every contribution builds an on-chain record: rounds paid, rounds missed, pots received, circles completed. It belongs to the member, not to a bank, and any lender or app can read it.

Why this needs Arc

One asset

On other chains a saver needs a second, volatile token just to move their dollars. On Arc, USDC is the gas, so a member's balance is all they need, and a relayer can be repaid in the same asset from the signed amount.

Fees you can quote

Gas is priced in dollars with a smoothed base fee, so the 0.005 USDC relay fee covers a contribution today and next month. A circle's cost is known before anyone joins.

Instant pots

Sub-second finality means the member whose turn it is has spendable dollars the moment the last share lands, not after confirmations.
